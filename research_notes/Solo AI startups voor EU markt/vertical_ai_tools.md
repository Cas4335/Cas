# Vertical / niche-industry AI tools run by 1–2 people (US market) and their Dutch/EU counterparts

Research date: 2026-11-24. Researcher notes for the report writer.

**Method caveat (read first):** Several primary sources could not be opened in this session because the hosts are outside its allowed network: trustmrr.com, indiehackers.com, bootstrappers.com, hackernoon.com and techcrunch.com. Facts from those sites come from search-result snippets/summaries, not full-page reads, and are marked "(snippet)". Almost all revenue figures below are **founder self-reported**. None were independently audited, and none could be checked against TrustMRR in this session.

**Headline finding:** Strictly verified examples of *US-focused, 1–2 person, vertical AI tools with ≥$10k MRR* are rarer than the "solo AI founder" hype suggests. Repeated targeted searches (contractors/trades, therapists, lawyers, AI receptionists, realtor listing copy) did **not** surface a single well-documented solo case with disclosed MRR in those exact verticals. The documented cases cluster in **real-estate imagery (virtual staging)**, **education (essay grading)**, **e-commerce/Amazon sellers** and **behavioral-health scribes** (the scribe case has 3 people).

## Which vertical AI micro-businesses have verified revenue of at least ~$10k MRR with 1–2 people?

### Takeaway
About 6 cases have credible (self-reported, dated) revenue of ≥$10k/month with a very small team: InteriorAI, Virtual Staging AI (since acquired by Zillow), EssayGrader, Launch Fast, Twofold (3 people, so just over the cap) and Pebblely (2 co-founders plus staff, revenue unverified). Another 3–4 are near the threshold or unverified (IACrea, Visualizee, Litmas AI, a UK virtual-staging tool). No case is independently audited, and several have exited or grown past 2 people.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The "1–2 person, $10k+ MRR" vertical AI cases that are actually documented skew towards **visual output (staging, product photos)** and **high-volume repetitive paperwork (essay grading, clinical notes)**. In those areas the AI output is immediately judgeable by the buyer, and a wrapper on top of image or LLM models can be shipped fast.
- Many successful cases **outgrow the 1–2 person definition or exit** (Virtual Staging AI → Zillow; EssayGrader bought, then grown; Twofold at 3; Pebblely at 4). This suggests that ~$1M ARR is roughly where solo vertical AI tools hire or sell.
- Launch Fast is the strongest evidence that a **non-technical domain insider plus Cursor plus a borrowed distribution channel** can hit $10k MRR in about 30 days. Domain knowledge and distribution mattered more than code.
- Contractors, AI receptionists and PI law look **crowded with funded companies or SMB-platform incumbents** (Jobber, EvenUp). That likely explains why no solo successes surfaced there.

### Gaps
- TrustMRR could not be browsed (host blocked). Its verified, Stripe-connected listings would be the best source of hard numbers and should be checked for categories such as real estate, education, health and e-commerce.
- No primary-source current (2026) revenue for InteriorAI, EssayGrader or Launch Fast. Pebblely and Litmas revenue is unverified.
- Launch dates and tech stacks for InteriorAI, EssayGrader and Twofold were not confirmed.
- No verified solo US case found for: restaurants (menus, review replies), recruiters, insurance agents, accountants/bookkeepers, coaches, or listing-description generators for realtors.

## Why do vertical tools work so well for solo founders (willingness to pay, low churn, clear channel)?

### Takeaway
The evidence points mainly to **clear, cheap acquisition channels** (SEO on profession-specific keywords, word of mouth among peers, partnerships with existing communities or coaches), plus **higher price points** than horizontal consumer tools ($125/case, $199/mo, per-property pricing). No hard churn data was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Word of mouth inside a profession:** EssayGrader grew "largely through word-of-mouth" to "100,000+ teachers" — (snippet) [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/essaygrader-ai)
- **SEO on niche keywords:** IACrea lists SEO and word of mouth as growth channels — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/iacrea). Visualizee's founder credits SEO for escaping ~$150/month: "SEO keeps working while you're at your day job" — (snippet) [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-150-month-to-8-6k-mrr-how-one-pivot-and-a-lot-of-seo-saved-my-ai-startup-2af6a82ee6)
- **Borrowed distribution (partnerships with communities, coaches or associations):** Launch Fast grew by partnering with Legacy X, an Amazon-seller coaching company, instead of building an audience — (snippet) [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-a-product-in-48-hours-and-hitting-30k-mrr-as-a-non-technical-founder-wWtWIH5tmwASUbxKaLT9)
- **Domain-insider founders:** Launch Fast's founder ran two Amazon brands. EssayGrader was built for a teacher spouse. Litmas's CEO ran a litigation firm for 10+ years — same sources above; [litmas.ai/about](https://litmas.ai/about/)
- **Willingness to pay (pricing signals):** Litmas from $125/case/month — [litmas.ai](https://litmas.ai/about/). Launch Fast $199/month list price — (snippet) [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-a-product-in-48-hours-and-hitting-30k-mrr-as-a-non-technical-founder-wWtWIH5tmwASUbxKaLT9). InteriorAI up to $299/month — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/interior-ai/about). Legal SaaS idea benchmarks of $99–199/lawyer/month are speculative — [aistartups.me](https://www.aistartups.me/9-ai-legal-tech-startup-ideas-for-solo-founders/)
- **Narrow wedge, then expand:** Twofold "started with a focused wedge around documentation" before broadening — (snippet) [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/lang/mn/twofold-ceo-we-built-a-multi-million-arr-healthcare-ai-company-with-three-people-and-zero-vc-money)
- **Generic advice from founders:** "a general solution page... won't work unless you focus on SEO and persist for more than 2 months" — (snippet) [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-i-made-10k-mrr-in-3-months-for-my-last-ai-saas-solo-eeadd1c071)
- **Benchmark (low quality):** median profitable micro-SaaS MRR is ~$4,200/month, attributed to Freemius 2025 via a roundup — [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/articles/best-micro-saas-ideas-solopreneurs)

### Inferences
- Vertical tools give solo founders a **searchable, nameable audience** ("virtual staging for realtors", "AI essay grader") and **concentrated watering holes** (coaching programs, teacher networks, bar/realtor associations). This reduces the need for paid ads.
- Buyers are professionals who bill for their time or sell high-ticket items (houses, legal cases). Time saved therefore converts directly into money, which supports $50–$300/month pricing that consumers would not pay.
- The risk side is that the same visibility attracts funded competitors and platform incumbents (Zillow buying Virtual Staging AI; Jobber and Funda building their own AI features). Moats come from workflow integration and domain trust, not the model.

### Gaps
- **No churn or retention data was found for any case.** The claim that vertical tools have "low churn" is not supported by evidence gathered here.
- No data on CAC or conversion rates per channel (Facebook groups, trade associations, cold email). No case explicitly credited Facebook groups or cold email.

## Which of these professions exist with the same pain points in the Netherlands/EU, and where are Dutch-language solutions missing?

### Takeaway
Every profession exists in NL, but the gaps differ. **Virtual staging and GP/medical scribes are already crowded** in NL/EU (Funda's own AI styling tool, GenRoom, IACrea; Juvoly, OurMind, Delphyr, Autoscriber). **Legal AI** has funded Dutch players and incumbents. The clearest **open gaps for Dutch-language solo tools** appear in **AI quotes for aannemers/installateurs**, **vertical AI receptionists for Dutch trades**, **Dutch secondary-school grading (with regulatory caveats)** and **Dutch psychologist/ggz-specific notes**.

### Cited Findings — per-company EU/NL assessment

### Inferences
- **Most promising NL gaps for a solo, Dutch-language vertical tool** (based on the absence of dedicated tools in searches, not proof of no demand):
  1. AI quote/offerte drafting for small aannemers, installateurs and hoveniers, sold via Techniek Nederland / Bouwend Nederland events.
  2. Vertical Dutch voice receptionist for trades, with dispatch and offerte booking. This will need to beat KPN's bundled offer on niche workflow.
  3. A Dutch, kerndoelen-aligned feedback and lesson assistant for VO/MBO, with "teacher-in-the-loop" by design.
  4. Psychologist/ggz-specific note tooling, which is high-regulation and slower to sell.
- **Avoid as a solo entrant:** virtual staging (Funda now competes directly), GP scribes (funded Dutch players plus 36% adoption) and generic e-commerce photo tools.
- Small language markets (NL ~18M people) cap the revenue ceiling. Combining NL with Flanders (Belgium), which shares the language, partially offsets this. Buildwise and Tooli show Flemish construction is a parallel market.

### Gaps
- Only one Dutch search was run per vertical. Dutch startup databases (Emerce, StartupJuncture, Sprout) were not systematically checked, so unknown small Dutch competitors probably exist, especially in offerte tools and AI receptionists.
- No data on Dutch makelaar or aannemer willingness to pay for AI tools.
- How Dutch civil procedure (more limited document production than US discovery) and the rarity of US-style contingency fees affect transferability of US legal AI products was not researched with sources. This is background knowledge only and needs verification.
- Notarissen and accountants/bookkeepers (Dutch: boekhouders, with Moneybird, e-Boekhouden and similar incumbents) were not researched due to time limits.

## Regulatory issues for EU (GDPR, AI Act risk categories for e.g. hiring/HR, medical data, Dutch professional rules)?

### Takeaway
Hiring/HR and education-grading tools fall into **AI Act Annex III high-risk** categories. A provisional political deal would move their obligations from **2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027**, but formal adoption was not confirmed in sources found. Medical scribes face health-data rules and Dutch professional-body guidance. Lawyers face NOvA AI recommendations, and makelaars face Funda's AI-image rules and misleading-advertising risk. EEA data hosting and "no training on customer data" are de facto market requirements.

### Cited Findings
- **AI Act high-risk timing:** stand-alone Annex III systems (employment, education, law enforcement) would move from **2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027** (a 16-month deferral). Systems embedded in Annex I products move to **2 August 2028** — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-); [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmoveo6ke00icuxskpgueb0od/ai-omnibus-update); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/eu-legislators-agree-to-delay-for-highrisk-ai-rules)
- **Adoption caveat:** if the Omnibus was not formally adopted before 2 Aug 2026, the original high-risk obligations apply from that date. A DLA Piper update of 30 June 2026 still called the change "proposed" — [DLA Piper, 2026-06-30](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act). The Omnibus would also remove the registration requirement for Annex III providers claiming a non-high-risk exemption — [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/digital-ai-omnibus-delays-key-deadlines-introduces-new-rules/)
- The AI Act is a regulation and applies directly in NL without transposition. NL only designates supervisors — (snippet) [search summary of Dutch legal sources](https://www.advocatie.nl/?p=90342)
- **Education:** the Dutch Ministry of Education says schools remain responsible for assessing student work, even with AI grading — [NOS](https://nos.nl/l/2609282)
- **Healthcare (NL):** a study of 535 GP consultations stresses that AI must not undermine confidentiality and that patients must be informed beforehand — [NL Times](https://nltimes.nl/node/90978). NOS reports a "wild growth" of medical digital tools with little oversight — [NOS](https://nos.nl/l/2612310). Juvoly's marketing stresses no recording or storage and NL/EEA hosting as a selling point — [Juvoly PDF](https://juvoly.nl/_files/ugd/c3cc57_f479787f8dbd40baaac097637459efa0.pdf)
- **Psychologists:** the NIP note says the psychologist remains accountable and must be critical about what is outsourced to AI — [NIP PDF](https://nip.nl/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/BEZ-notitie_Gebruik-AI-in-de-psychologiepraktijk_def-Kopie.pdf)
- **Lawyers (NL):** the Nederlandse Orde van Advocaten has issued "Aanbevelingen AI in de advocatuur" — (snippet) [ICTRecht ASL brochure](https://www.ictrecht.nl/hubfs/Opleidingsbrochures/ASL%20leerweg/ASL-Brochure.pdf). Market baseline: EEA processing and no training on inputs — [ICTRecht study](https://www.ictrecht.nl/hubfs/Kennisdocumenten/Onderzoek/AI-tooling%20voor%20de%20legal%20professionals/Onderzoek%20AI-tooling%20voor%20de%20legal%20professionals.pdf). (US comparison: ABA Formal Opinion 512 stresses confidentiality when lawyers use AI — [LawPractice.ai](https://www.lawpractice.ai/blog/best-ai-demand-letter-software))
- **Makelaars:** Funda bans AI images that change reality. Consumer programme Radar flagged possible misleading advertising — [Hart van Nederland](https://www.hartvannederland.nl/economie/wonen/artikelen/ai-fotos-funda-misleidend-woningaanbod-makelaars); [Radar](https://radar.avrotros.nl/artikel/geven-ai-fotos-op-funda-een-onrealistisch-beeld-kan-sprake-zijn-van-misleiding-62904). Funda also raises the open question of liability and data handling with external AI tools — (snippet) [Funda voor makelaars](https://www.funda.nl/voormakelaars/artikel-makelaar/welke-kansen-en-bedreigingen-biedt-ai/). IACrea publishes guidance on whether virtual staging must be declared — [IACrea](https://www.iacrea.com/en/blog/virtual-home-staging/virtual-home-staging-is-it-legal-and-should-it-be-declared)
- **Installers/contractors:** Techniek Nederland flags liability for damage from AI decisions and calculation errors in AI-generated quotes — [Techniek Nederland PDF](https://www.technieknederland.nl/media/l3tjeo2c/260414-tn-regiobijeenkomst-oost-handout.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Regulatory difficulty for an EU solo founder, from highest to lowest:** HR/recruiting screening (Annex III high-risk) ≈ grading or admissions in education (Annex III high-risk) > clinical scribes (special-category health data under GDPR, professional-body rules, possible medical-device questions) > legal (confidentiality, NOvA guidance) > real estate (misleading-advertising rules, Funda policy) > trades quotes and e-commerce listings (mostly ordinary GDPR and liability). The last group is therefore the most solo-friendly in the EU.
- Grading tools can reduce AI Act exposure by keeping a teacher-in-the-loop and by not determining final grades. CitoLab's "always semi-automatic" stance hints at the norm. Whether this escapes the high-risk classification would need legal review.
- Background (not sourced in this session, verify before use): health data is a GDPR Art. 9 special category requiring explicit legal bases and usually a DPIA. US tools built on US-hosted LLM APIs would need EU hosting and DPAs to sell in NL.

### Gaps
- Whether the AI Omnibus was formally adopted in the Official Journal before or after 2 Aug 2026 could not be confirmed. As of today (2026-11-24) the report writer should treat the high-risk timeline as uncertain.
- Whether AI scribes count as medical devices under the EU MDR, and how Dutch supervisors (AP, IGJ) treat them, was not researched.
- Specific Dutch rules for notarissen (KNB) and accountants (NBA) regarding AI were not researched.
- Hosts blocked in this session (trustmrr.com, indiehackers.com, bootstrappers.com, hackernoon.com, techcrunch.com) would need to be allowed for full-text verification of the revenue claims above.
