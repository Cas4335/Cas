# Solo AI-built non-SaaS online businesses (directories, job boards, newsletters, digital products, data, POD/Etsy, faceless video, productized services) and their NL/EU replicability

Research date: 2026-11-24. Method: web search only. In this cloud session WebFetch could not reach trustmrr.com or dirstarter.com (host not in allowed network domains), so TrustMRR figures come from search snippets and secondary profiles, not the verified TrustMRR pages. Most revenue figures below are founder self-reports or third-party write-ups. Each one says how reliable it is.

Note on the "US" constraint: the best-documented "solo, non-SaaS, built-in-public" businesses are often run by non-US founders selling mainly to US/global buyers (Marc Lou is French, Pieter Levels is Dutch, Easlo is Singapore-based, Piotr Kulpinski/OpenAlternative is Polish [prior knowledge, not verified this session]). They are included because they sell to the US/English-speaking market. Each one is flagged.

---

## Q1: Which models make solo operators the most money in 2025–2026, with verified figures? (Business profiles)

### Takeaway
The best-documented solo money-makers in 2025–2026 are founder-led portfolios that combine a **boilerplate/digital product + build-in-public audience** (Marc Lou: ~$1.03M total 2025 revenue, self-reported) and **job boards/community directories with paid listings** (Pieter Levels' RemoteOK ~$41K/month, May 2025). **Productized services** (Designjoy, ~$145K MRR Feb 2025, secondary source) are the highest-revenue solo model in absolute terms, but they are not really "AI-built". Niche directories that sell featured listings usually earn a few hundred to ~$13K/month. AI newsletters only reach millions once they hire a team (The Rundown: $3M in 2024). Etsy AI downloads and faceless AI YouTube have big unverified anecdotes and growing platform risk.

### Inferences
- Rough ranking of solo money potential (2025–2026, documented cases): productized service (Designjoy, high but labor-bound) > founder-brand digital products/boilerplates plus portfolio (Marc Lou ~$1M/yr total) > established job board (RemoteOK ~$0.5M/yr) > Notion/template products (~$0.2–0.5M/yr at peak, mostly 2022–2023) > niche directories ($1K–13K/month) > AI Etsy and faceless YouTube (anecdotes only, high platform risk).
- The common factor among the top earners is an **owned audience** (X/YouTube/newsletter), not AI. AI and vibe coding lower build cost, so distribution becomes the moat.
- Marc Lou's 2025 revenue was reportedly ~20% *lower* than 2024. Even the poster-child boilerplate business is not growing, which suggests the boilerplate niche is saturating as AI coding tools make boilerplates less necessary.
- Newsletters scale far past solo revenue, but only after hiring (The Rundown). Solo AI newsletters top out at roughly sponsorship pricing × send frequency (e.g. $2K/main slot at Ben's Bites).

- No reliable solo-era revenue for The Rundown or Ben's Bites.

## Q2: Which models are being killed by AI search (Google AI Overviews) and which are resilient?

### Takeaway
Informational, ad-monetized content sites and low-ranked programmatic pages are hit hardest (reports of 40–70% traffic loss for small publishers). Commercial-intent sites and models with direct demand or owned distribution (job boards, transactional directories, newsletters, digital products sold through an audience, productized services) are much more resilient. AI-generated "slop" on platforms (YouTube, Etsy) faces a separate threat: platform policy, not search.

### Cited Findings
- AI Overviews (launched May 2024) reportedly cut traffic by up to 70% for some publishers (general article, not a controlled study) — [Playwire](https://www.playwire.com/blog/resources/blog/google-ai-overviews-are-gutting-publisher-traffic); [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/ai-overviews-solutions)
- Google referral traffic fell ~60% over two years for small sites with 1,000–10,000 daily pageviews — [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/report-ai-overviews-are-crushing-small-publishers-as-google-search-traffic-drops-60)
- Independent and specialist sites report drops of up to 40% where AI Overviews have fully rolled out. Positions 4–10 reportedly saw up to 50% CTR drops — [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/google-ai-mode-causing-web-chaos-traffic-drop-can-your-site-recover-1739603); [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/google-ai-overviews-slash-website-traffic-by-50-for-small-businesses)
- Counterpoint: the Website Planet study found *commercial* websites gained an average of 153% organic traffic, while informational sites were mixed (one saw −76%) — [Website Planet](https://websiteplanet.com/blog/ai-summaries-website-impact-research)
- AI Overview referral traffic mostly goes to big players (TripAdvisor, Wikipedia, Mayo Clinic, YouTube) — [BrightEdge via Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/one-year-in-seo-lessons-from-publishers-after-googles-ai-overviews)
- Publishers describe a "reckoning" for open-web traffic — [AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/publishers/the-ai-search-reckoning-is-dismantling-open-web-traffic-and-publishers-may-never-recover); website makers feel "betrayed" — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/04/09/google-ai-search-shift-leaves-website-makers-feeling-betrayed)
- Directory revenue that comes from *featured listings and sponsorships paid by listed businesses* (not display ads) depends less on raw traffic volume. OpenAlternative's revenue is 65% sponsorships and 35% featured listings — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/2KasH9NLK3ehlTL9GeGO)
- Platform risk for AI content: YouTube's inauthentic-content policy (July 2025, tightened 2026) — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/). Etsy art/craft category "hit hard by AI saturation" — [Deadline Funnel](https://www.deadlinefunnel.com/blog/etsy-digital-products)

### Inferences
- **Most at risk:** programmatic-SEO pages answering informational queries ("best X for Y", "what is", comparisons, AI tool listicles) monetized by display ads or affiliate links. Mass AI-generated directories with thin content (e.g. generic AI tool directories, of which there are thousands) also fall here.
- **Moderately at risk:** generic AI-tools directories. Their traffic is informational and AI assistants already answer "which AI tool for X". Flippa valuations (~14x monthly profit) suggest buyers price in this risk.
- **Resilient:** (a) job boards: a job seeker must click through to apply, and employers pay to post. (b) Transactional/local directories where users want to contact or book a provider. (c) Newsletters and communities, where the email list is owned distribution. (d) Digital products and boilerplates sold through a personal brand. (e) Productized services, sold through outbound/referral/X. (f) Verified data products (TrustMRR-style) whose value is proprietary data that LLMs cannot generate.
- **Killed by platforms rather than search:** faceless AI YouTube built on templated content, and low-effort AI Etsy art.
- These are inferences from general publisher data. No source measured directories or programmatic SEO specifically.

### Gaps
- No direct dataset on AI Overviews' impact on directory or job-board traffic specifically.
- No data on AI Overviews' rollout and impact in Dutch-language search (NL), so it is unknown whether NL is lagging or equally affected.

## Q3: Which can be replicated for the Dutch/EU audience, what equivalents exist, and where are the gaps?

### Takeaway
English-language global models (boilerplates, AI tool directories, AI newsletters) are effectively **saturated in EU**, because EU buyers use the English originals and several are built by EU founders. **Dutch-language** versions exist only thinly. There are a few small Dutch AI tool directories and small Dutch AI Substacks, but I found no established Dutch-language niche directories with featured listings, no Dutch AI newsletter at scale with public numbers, and no verified Dutch niche job boards in most verticals. These look like **some competition / open gaps**, limited by NL's small market size (~18M people, plus ~6.5M Dutch speakers in Flanders [prior knowledge]).

### Cited Findings
- Dutch AI tool directories exist: **AI Tools NL / aitoolhub.nl** ("tested and published guides for well over 100 tools") — [DEV Community (aukedh)](https://dev.to/aukedh/how-to-choose-the-right-ai-tool-in-2026-a-guide-for-dutch-users-3lkd); **AIToolly NL** (machine-translated international directory, ~2,585 tools) — [aitoolly.com/nl](https://aitoolly.com/nl); RankMyAI Dutch AI sector report (1,200+ NL AI companies, top 250 ranking) — [rankmyai.com](https://www.rankmyai.com/insights/5); GetApp NL (Dutch software comparison) — [getapp.nl](https://www.getapp.nl/directory/1397/artificial-intelligence/deployment-options/windows/software?page=2)
- Dutch AI newsletters are small: a Dutch Substack on AI at work has >3,400 subscribers — [fmthandpickedai.substack.com](https://fmthandpickedai.substack.com/p/deel-je-ai-ervaringen-inspireer-anderen); also [joosten.substack.com](https://joosten.substack.com/p/deze-ai-tools-gebruik-ik-in-mijn). Dutch "best AI newsletter" listicles recommend almost exclusively English titles — [ClickUp NL](https://clickup.com/nl/blog/120025/beste-ai-nieuwsbrieven/); [readless.app/nl](https://www.readless.app/nl/blog/best-ai-newsletters-to-subscribe)
- EU boilerplate equivalent: **supastarter** by Jonathan Wilke (Germany), solo. ~$10K/month (Starter Story, Oct 2024) and **$8,718 in Aug 2026** (founder-reported) — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/supastarter-breakdown); [aiidelist](https://aiidelist.com/indie-developers/jonathan-wilke)
- Remote job boards covering NL are international and English-language: FlexJobs (~1,023 NL roles) [flexjobs.com](https://www.flexjobs.com/remote-jobs/world/netherlands), Jobgether (~21,575 NL jobs) [jobgether.com](https://jobgether.com/remote-jobs/netherlands), Built In EU [builtin.com](https://builtin.com/jobs/remote/eu/netherlands?page=13). Jobgether notes the Dutch government is tightening self-employment (ZZP) rules — [Jobgether](https://jobgether.com/remote-jobs/netherlands)
- Lead lists in the EU carry GDPR exposure (scraped personal contact data). This is noted only as a general caveat in search output, not a cited legal source — see Gaps.

### Inferences
- **Where Dutch-language SEO may be less competitive:** fewer competing pages per query and fewer pSEO operators target Dutch. The trade-off is lower search volume and lower total revenue ceilings. That is reasonable to infer, but no source measured Dutch keyword competition in this session.
- **Best replicable models for NL/EU (inference):** (1) Dutch-language vertical job boards (freelance/ZZP, healthcare, trades, tech-in-NL) with paid postings. These are resilient to AI Overviews because the user must click to apply. (2) Dutch/EU B2B niche directories that sell featured listings to vendors (e.g. EU-hosted/GDPR-compliant software alternatives, local service providers). (3) A business-focused Dutch AI newsletter monetized with Dutch B2B sponsors and workshops (The Rundown's mix of memberships + workshops + ads [Thairath](https://en.thairath.co.th/money/business_marketing/business_strategy/2908268)). (4) Dutch-localized templates/digital products tied to Dutch admin specifics.
- **Avoid for NL/EU:** generic English boilerplates, AI tool directories, faceless AI YouTube, AI Etsy art. These are saturated globally and EU buyers already use the English originals.
- **Distribution is the binding constraint.** Every top earner here has a large X/YouTube audience. A Dutch replicator needs a Dutch-language distribution channel (LinkedIn NL is likely more important than X for Dutch B2B [inference]).
