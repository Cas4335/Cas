# Solo/duo B2B & prosumer AI micro-SaaS (US market) and their EU/NL equivalents

Research date: 2026-11-24. Method note: WebFetch could not reach trustmrr.com, webscraperapi.ai or startupfounderstories.com (blocked in this session's network). All figures below come from search-result snippets of those and other pages, not full-page reads, so exact numbers and dates should be re-checked on the live TrustMRR pages before publishing. Most revenue figures are founder-reported; "Stripe-verified" means listed on TrustMRR (a Stripe-connected revenue database built by indie founder Marc Lou).

## 1. Which solo/duo AI SaaS products have publicly verified revenue of at least ~$10k MRR?

### Takeaway
About 10 horizontal AI/prosumer products run by 1–2 people (or close to it) clear $10k MRR. The strongest verified ones are Post Bridge (~$49k MRR, Stripe-verified Nov 2026), SEObot (~$30–35k MRR, Stripe-verified) and Sleek.design (~$23k MRR, verified Aug 2026). The largest are Photo AI, HeadshotPro and TypingMind ($100k+/month). Those three are self-reported only, and all three founders are non-US indie hackers selling mainly to US/global customers. Two of them, Pieter Levels and Danny Postma, are Dutch.

## 2. How were they built (no-code / vibe code / AI-assisted) and how fast?

### Takeaway
Build stories are mostly founder anecdotes: Sleek in about three weeks, Post Bridge vibe-coded with Cursor, HeyDATA on Replit, Plinq on Lovable in 45 days. The products with the most revenue (Photo AI, HeadshotPro, TypingMind) were built by experienced solo developers on simple stacks plus third-party AI APIs/models, not by non-coders. The "three weeks" claims usually hide reuse of earlier code.

### Cited Findings
- Sleek: "three-week build" that "reused four earlier design products". — [Sofarbot](https://www.sofarbot.com/stories/sleek-mattia-pomelli-10k-mrr-6-weeks)
- Post Bridge: vibe-coded with AI assistants and Cursor in 2024. — [Medium round-up](https://medium.com/@samyabratamaji828/these-vibe-coded-saas-products-make-10k-mrr-real-case-studies-8a2b5783892b)
- HeyDATA: founder without deep web-dev experience used Replit plus generative tooling. — [Medium round-up](https://medium.com/@samyabratamaji828/these-vibe-coded-saas-products-make-10k-mrr-real-case-studies-8a2b5783892b)
- Plinq: a non-technical founder built it in about 45 days on Lovable, reaching ~R$2.2M ARR (~$456k). It is a consumer safety product and Brazil-based, so it is out of scope, but it is a reference point for Lovable. — [Medium round-up](https://medium.com/@samyabratamaji828/these-vibe-coded-saas-products-make-10k-mrr-real-case-studies-8a2b5783892b)
- TrendFeed: an AI content discovery tool reportedly built in four days with Claude and GPT-4, hitting $10K MRR in month one. Unverified, single source. — [Medium round-up](https://medium.com/@samyabratamaji828/these-vibe-coded-saas-products-make-10k-mrr-real-case-studies-8a2b5783892b)
- Claimed tooling trend: "Claude Code used by 75% of 1–4 person teams" (attributed to Pragmatic Engineer 2026). Could not verify against the original. — [onepc.org](https://onepc.org/stats/ai-stack-solo-founders-2026)
- Chatbase's infrastructure is on Supabase (it started with a team of 5). — [Supabase customer story](https://supabase.com/customers/chatbase)
- Photo AI's own story: Levels had "70 failed startups" before it, i.e. a seasoned builder. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/how-one-photo-ai-app-generates-132k-monthly-after-70-failed-startups/)

### Inferences
- "Vibe coding" lowers build time but not distribution difficulty. The verified winners mostly already had an audience or previous products. Examples: Levels, Postma, Dinh, Rush (24+ products), Tibo, and Jack Friks with his content following.
- The technology moat is thin for wrappers such as headshots, ChatGPT UIs and SEO bots, so speed-to-market and distribution dominate. That makes an EU clone technically trivial, and the question becomes whether there is a localized distribution advantage.

### Gaps
- No primary source found detailing the exact stack (framework, model provider) for Photo AI, HeadshotPro, SEObot or TypingMind in this session. Background knowledge, unverified here: Levels uses PHP/jQuery/SQLite with Replicate-hosted models; TypingMind is a client-side web app on LLM APIs.

## 3. How did they acquire customers?

### Takeaway
The dominant channel is the founder's personal audience on X/Twitter ("build in public"), followed by SEO and programmatic SEO. Paid ads and cold outreach barely feature. Post Bridge combined a creator audience (YouTube/TikTok/X) with aggressive low pricing. Chatbase went viral from a single tweet.

### Cited Findings
- Chatbase: "$1M in 117 days from a tweet to 16 followers", i.e. viral X launch. — [Superframeworks case study](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/chatbase)
- Chatbase hit $1M ARR in under 5 months post-launch. — [Yespress](https://yespress.io/yasser-elsaid)
- Post Bridge: positioned by "undercutting bloated schedulers" on price. — [Startup Founder Stories](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/jack-friks-post-bridge-11k-mrr)
- Sleek: hit $10K MRR "without spending a single dollar on marketing". — [Medium round-up](https://medium.com/@samyabratamaji828/these-vibe-coded-saas-products-make-10k-mrr-real-case-studies-8a2b5783892b)
- Pattern across cases: every case-study founder who reached $10K MRR "talked to potential customers before and during building". — [search synthesis of vibe-coding case studies](https://blog.vibecoder.me/case-study-saas-zero-to-10k-mrr-with-ai)
- Danny Postma: "the product was boring on purpose" (solo AI headshot empire). He is known for SEO-driven growth. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/promptway/he-built-an-ai-headshot-empire-solo-the-product-was-boring-on-purpose-50i0); [Bootstrapped Founder](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/danny-postma-an-indie-hackers-business-evolution/)
- SEObot is sold to founders and indie makers via John Rush's own network of 24+ products and directories, i.e. cross-promotion. — [IndieMakerList](https://indiemakerlist.com/p/marc-lou/) (portfolio claim from aggregator)

### Inferences
- These channels (English X/Twitter, English Google SEO) are naturally global. That is why Dutch founders such as Levels and Postma sell to the US rather than NL.
- An NL/EU clone would lack the X build-in-public flywheel, which is much smaller in Dutch. It would rely on Dutch-language SEO (less competitive), LinkedIn, partnerships and reseller/agency channels.

### Gaps
- No quantified channel breakdown (e.g. % SEO vs X) found for any of the companies.

## 4. Do equivalents already exist in the Netherlands/EU (Dutch, German, French)?

Most categories already have EU competitors or big-tech players with Dutch-localized pages:
- **Headshots/photos:** saturated (Adobe, Canva, Picsart all have Dutch pages, and HeadshotPro itself is Dutch-founded).

## 5. What makes the US version succeed, and would it transfer to the EU?

### Takeaway
The US successes rest on:
- a single huge English-speaking market;
- frictionless card payments and self-serve subscriptions;
- X/Twitter build-in-public distribution;
- a willingness to buy cheap point tools.

In the EU, fragmented languages shrink each addressable market. Data protection (GDPR/DPA, EU hosting) and the AI Act add sales friction, but they also create a differentiator. NL buyers often expect iDEAL/SEPA payment and Dutch invoices with VAT. The transferable opportunities are text/language-heavy B2B tools sold as "EU-hosted, GDPR, Dutch-native".

### Cited Findings
- EU buyers care about data residency. Chatbase alternatives market explicitly on EU hosting and DPAs. — [WebChatAgent](https://webchatagent.com/compare/chatbase-alternative); [Boei](https://boei.help/blog/best-chatbase-alternatives/)
- However, "GDPR does not require every chatbot to store data only in the EU". EU hosting is a selling point, not a legal requirement. — [SiteGPT GDPR article](https://sitegpt.ai/blog/gdpr-compliant-chatbot-platforms)
- Langdock's growth in Germany is built on "DSGVO-compliant wrapper" positioning, and it is priced above ChatGPT Plus. This shows a willingness to pay for compliance in DACH. — [basic thinking](https://www.basicthinking.de/blog/2026/03/26/langdock-deutsche-alternative-chatgpt/)
- European-alternative directories (europeanpurpose.com, AlternativeTo "EU only" filter) exist and rank "made in EU" tools. ### Inferences
- Market size: NL is about 18M people. A solo tool at $49/month needs ~200 customers for $10k MRR. That is feasible in NL B2B for SEO, content or support tools, but not for consumer-ish photo tools that compete with free Canva/Adobe features.
- The best transfer candidates are SEObot-style (Dutch SEO agent for WordPress/Yoast and Shopify/Lightspeed shops) and TypingMind-style (Dutch, EU-hosted multi-LLM team workspace for SMBs, competing below Langdock's price). Both are weakly verified as gaps.
- Poor transfer candidates are headshots, AI photos and social schedulers (saturated, global players localized).
- Payment habits (iDEAL, SEPA, invoices with BTW) and GDPR paperwork (DPA, subprocessors list) are table stakes a Dutch clone must offer, and they are a moat against US indie tools that ignore them. This is an inference from competitors' positioning, not a measured buyer survey.

### Gaps
- No data found on Dutch SMB willingness-to-pay for AI SaaS, iDEAL share in B2B SaaS payments, or EU AI Act obligations for these tool types. Further research is needed.
- No revenue data found for any EU clone (Boei, Langdock ARR, etc.), so EU market depth is unquantified.
